# Nurture pipeline — tentative design inputs

**Status: open design.** These are Tom's first-cut ideas, not an approved
stage list. No pipeline stages or automation are settled by this note.

## Tentative inputs from Tom

- During days 0–7 after a quote, the work is to convert the offer. The quote
  and contract expire at day 7.
- Long-term nurture follows as a separate broad phase.

These inputs name two kinds of work and an expiry point. They do not yet define
pipeline stages, a follow-up schedule, or the event that moves a prospect
between phases.

## Decisions and triggers still needed

- What event starts day 0: quote drafted, sent, received, or acknowledged?
- Does “day 7” mean the end of the seventh calendar day or exactly 168 hours?
  Which timezone controls the deadline, and are quote and contract expiry
  always the same instant?
- What does “convert the offer” mean for this pipeline: signed contract,
  deposit received, first payment, service start, or another confirmed event?
- What events stop the conversion work early, such as a signed agreement,
  explicit decline, withdrawal, or an expired quote? What should happen to the
  card after each outcome?
- Which follow-up actions belong in this phase, how often may they happen, and
  what are the allowed channels? Front Desk owns outbound contact; this note
  does not authorize or schedule messages.
- What event starts long-term nurture: quote expiry, an explicit “not now”, or
  another decision? Should every unconverted prospect enter it?
- What cadence, duration, re-entry rules, and stop conditions should long-term
  nurture use? What response or signal should bring a prospect back to active
  work?
- Should these two kinds of work be separate pipelines or phases in one
  pipeline? Which brands and lead types are in scope?
- Which parts should be tracked as pipeline columns, and which should be
  reminders or tasks? What should remain manual until the process is proven?

## Boundary

Do not turn these inputs into a final stage list or implementation until Tom
settles the open decisions and the design note receives review acceptance.
